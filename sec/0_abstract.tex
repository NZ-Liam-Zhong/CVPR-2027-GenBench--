\begin{abstract}
Evaluating zero-shot robot policies requires diverse tasks, explicit embodiment interfaces, and evidence that simulated outcomes are informative about physical execution. We introduce GenBench-$\infty$, a benchmark that derives robot evaluation tasks from egocentric videos of human activities. The initial benchmark comprises 1,000 scenes and 3,000 task instances. An agentic real-to-simulation pipeline constructs interactive environments, while asynchronous execution supports evaluation of systems with different inference costs. We distinguish a shared 50-scene evaluation subset from full-suite evaluations and paired simulation--real-world tests.
